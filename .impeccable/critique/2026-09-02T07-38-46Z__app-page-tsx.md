## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Genuinely authored for a real 1967 Cyprus jewellery boutique, not a reskinned template — Greek product names, correct "monopetra" engagement-ring terminology, a real Latsia address wired to a live Maps link and live open/closed badge, a real phone number, EUR pricing, and a deliberately non-SaaS typographic system (Fraunces italic serif + uppercase-tracked rectangular buttons). The risk is structural rather than content-level: ~8 of 13 sections share the identical eyebrow → italic h2 → paragraph → single CTA anatomy, so a fast scroller sees a templated skeleton even though the words are bespoke.

**Deterministic scan**: `detect.mjs --json app/page.tsx components/home` (12 files) — **clean, exit 0, zero findings**. No slop patterns (gradient text, emoji icons, generic shadows) mechanically flagged. This corroborates the LLM's read that nothing here is lazily generic at the component level.

**Visual overlays**: Not available this run — no browser/DOM-automation tool was exposed in either sub-agent's toolset, so the live-server injection overlay and 1440px/375px screenshots could not be produced. Both assessments worked from source (JSX/Tailwind/data) rather than rendered pixels. Fallback signal only; no user-visible overlay exists to point to.

## Overall Impression

The homepage is real, well-crafted, and unmistakably *this* boutique's — the hero has genuine craft (parallax, staggered reveal, one-shot gold shimmer, magnetic CTA), and `BoutiqueLocation` is a standout trust section. The single biggest opportunity is the middle of the page: four to five sections in a row share one template shape, and one interactive feature (Shop the Look hotspots) points at nothing a customer can actually see, which is the one moment that could break trust rather than build it.

## What's Working

1. **`PlaceholderArt` / `.marble-surface` system** — a coherent, on-brand fine-line-on-marble treatment used consistently as the "no photography yet" fallback. Reads as a deliberate choice, not a broken image, and swapping in real photos needs zero further code changes.
2. **`BoutiqueLocation`** — real address on a live Maps directions link, live open/closed badge, click-to-call. The section that most convincingly proves "this is a real Cyprus shop."
3. **Restrained, purposeful motion** — the hero's one-shot gold-shimmer (fires once, never loops), magnetic CTA, and `Reveal` entrance system show real motion discipline; nothing gratuitous.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Shop-the-Look hotspots point at nothing.** `CampaignFeature` overlays 3 hotspot pins at hardcoded coordinates on top of a single abstract `PlaceholderArt` line-icon, with copy reading "Tap the marks to shop the pieces shown." There's nothing at those coordinates a customer can identify — a real visitor taps a pin floating over empty marble texture and gets a product card for something they never saw. This is the one place placeholder art reads as *broken* rather than *tasteful*.
- **Why it matters**: It's the highest-trust-risk moment on the page — an interaction that visibly doesn't work.
- **Fix**: Drop the hotspot pattern from the homepage until real campaign photography exists; replace with a plain CTA + a row of the 3 featured product thumbnails beneath the copy.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden` or targeted fix in `campaign-feature.tsx`

**[P1] Newsletter signup is a no-op that fakes success.** `newsletter-form.tsx` only sets local state and shows a success toast — no API call or storage anywhere. A visitor's email is silently discarded on refresh while the UI confidently confirms it worked.
- **Why it matters**: CLAUDE.md already bans fabricating a "payment succeeded" state beyond what's implemented — this is the identical failure mode for lead capture, and it's a permanently lost customer contact.
- **Fix**: Wire to a real destination (even a minimal Supabase `subscribers` table) before launch, or mark the field "coming soon."
- **Suggested command**: not a visual command — flagged for a data-layer fix, not part of this design pass

**[P1] Midsection template repetition.** `HeritageStory`, `EngagementFeature`, `BespokeEditorial`, and `BoutiqueLocation` all use the identical eyebrow → serif h2 → paragraph → single CTA skeleton in a 12-col text/icon split, alternating sides; `CraftServices` repeats the eyebrow+h2 pattern again right after. 4-5 near-identical section shapes in sequence is the exact "could be any template" pattern the specificity check exists to catch.
- **Why it matters**: Creates the mid-page emotional valley the review flagged, and undercuts the otherwise strong specificity verdict.
- **Fix**: Structurally differentiate at least two sections — e.g. turn Bespoke's 4-step process into a real horizontal stepper/timeline instead of a 2x2 grid that visually matches Heritage's stat block.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable bolder` or `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] Three groups exceed the ≤4 chunking guideline.** `CuratedCategories` (6 tiles), `CraftServices` (6 of 8 shown), `InstagramSection` (6 tiles) each present 6 same-weight items; Craft Services collapses to a single column of 6 near-identical rows below `sm:`.
- **Why it matters**: Pushes past comfortable working-memory limits, especially on mobile where it's most likely skimmed past.
- **Fix**: Trim the homepage teaser to 3-4 services (link "All Services" for the rest); consider a tighter mobile grid for Instagram.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout`

**[P3] Weak closing beat.** The page's last section before the footer is the (currently non-functional) newsletter ask rather than `BoutiqueLocation`'s stronger in-person-visit trust moment.
- **Why it matters**: Peak-end rule — the page should close on its strongest beat, not a generic email capture.
- **Fix**: Reorder so the physical-visit CTA is the structural close, or shrink Newsletter's visual weight to read as footer-adjacent utility.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout`

**[P3] Hero watermark may compete with the headline.** The giant `MAVRIKIOS` ghost wordmark sits behind the floating jewellery cards and above the h1 in the same viewport — worth confirming live it doesn't fragment attention in the critical under-3-second window.
- **Why it matters**: First-viewport clarity is the highest-leverage real estate on the page.
- **Fix**: Verify live at 1440px; reduce opacity/scale further if it competes.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable polish`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (confused first-timer, never bought fine jewellery online)**: Hits the Engagement category tile on mobile and sees "monopetra" with zero context (description text is `hidden` below `sm:`). Reaches the Campaign section and is told to "tap the marks to shop the pieces shown" — taps a pin over an abstract icon with nothing recognizable shown (the P0 above). No sizing/returns/exchange reassurance anywhere on the page for a big-ticket first purchase, and every image on the page is a line drawing rather than a photo — no photographic proof of a real shop before clicking through.

**Casey (distracted mobile user, one-handed thumb)**: Real scroll distance before content (announcement bar + sticky nav + full-height hero). `ProductRail`'s snap-scroll is genuinely well-built for one-handed swiping. `CraftServices`' 6-row single column at 375px is a long uninterrupted scroll of near-identical rows 8 sections deep. The hero marquee only pauses on `group-hover`, which never fires on touch, so Casey can't pause it to read an individual message mid-scroll.

## Minor Observations

- `HeritageStory`'s "years of craftsmanship" is computed from `siteConfig.since`, staying accurate without manual updates — preserve this in any redesign.
- `ReviewsSection`'s Google link opens a bare search query rather than a verified Business Profile URL — fine as a stopgap, swap once available.
- The ✦ ticker glyph and lucide-only icon set are consistent, non-emoji choices — a real anti-generic-AI signal worth explicitly preserving.
- `ShopTheLookHotspot`'s ping-ring affordance is well-built in isolation — it's the thing it's pointing at (P0) that's the problem, not the interaction itself.
- Badge variants (New/Limited/Best Seller) on `ProductCard` are a clean, non-cluttered state signal.

## Questions to Consider

- What if the Campaign/Shop-the-Look section is simply held back until real photography exists, rather than shipping a broken-feeling interaction on top of a placeholder never designed to support hotspots?
- What if 2-3 "hero moments" (Heritage, Bespoke) got genuinely distinct treatments and the rest collapsed into one denser "Our Craft" section — cutting 13 sections toward 8-9 and fixing both the chunking and midsection-valley findings at once?
- What if the newsletter form were visibly marked "coming soon" until wired to something real — would that honesty read as more trustworthy than a working-looking form that silently discards the email?
